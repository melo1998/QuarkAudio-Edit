Abstract

Text-guided audio editing aims to modify input audio according to natural-language instructions. Existing systems often support limited operation types and struggle with free-form instructions involving multi-step edits. We present QuarkAudio-Edit, a unified framework that supports multi-step edits by decoupling atomic planning from diffusion-based signal editing. The hybrid planner maps free-form instructions to atomic steps drawn from six operation types, using either direct template retrieval or model-based chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. The Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT) editor then executes the resulting plan via conditional diffusion while preserving non-target content. The framework is trained on data from multi-source synthesis pipelines and further refined through progressive reinforcement learning over planning quality and acoustic fidelity. Beyond sound-event editing, the same architecture also supports word-level speech editing and standalone text-to-audio generation. On our complex declarative editing test set, QuarkAudio-Edit reduces Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD) by 21.2% over the strong baseline SmartDJ (3.27 vs. 4.15), and achieves the best human-rated relevance and content preservation scores.

Overview

QuarkAudio-Edit addresses four limitations of existing text-guided audio editing (TAE) systems: uneven support for attribute-level controls (loudness, speed) and temporal ordering, inability to interpret declarative high-level instructions, absence of structured atomic planning with explicit CoT traces, and the lack of quality-aware reinforcement learning for planning and editing.

Given a declarative instruction such as “Turn this recording of birds chirping over traffic noise into a rainy evening café scene”, the hybrid planner decomposes it into a sequence of interpretable atomic operations from the set {Add, Remove, Replace, Speed, Loudness, Order} — either by direct template retrieval (cosine similarity ≥ τ = 0.6 over a 550K+ index) or by model-based CoT reasoning — which the MMDiT diffusion editor executes sequentially on the source audio.

  • 6 atomic operations — spanning event, attribute, and temporal dimensions
  • Hybrid planning — direct retrieval over a 550K+ RAG index, or CoT reasoning
  • Two-stage progressive GRPO — planning quality then acoustic fidelity
  • One architecture, three tasks — sound-event editing, word-level speech editing, and text-to-audio generation

Method

A decoupled Planner–Editor architecture that separates what to edit (high-level reasoning) from how to edit (signal-level synthesis).

Figure 2. Architecture of QuarkAudio-Edit. The hybrid planner decomposes the declarative instruction into atomic steps, either by retrieval or model-based reasoning, while the MMDiT editor executes them sequentially. The Planner and Editor modules are trained separately and the former is optimized by the LoRA technique.

Data Synthesis Pipeline

Three complementary methods produce over 500,000 triplets (150K offline plus ~350K composed online per epoch): Prompt-to-Prompt synthesis, deterministic signal-level edits, and online composition from AudioSet and VGG-Sound, plus ~50K CoT instruction–decomposition pairs.

Hybrid RAPT Planner

Direct template retrieval over a 550K+ FAISS HNSW index (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, 1024-d, <50 ms): if cosine similarity ≥ τ = 0.6 the matched atomic sequence is returned directly; otherwise a Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct planner (LoRA r=64, ~38M trainable) performs model-based CoT reasoning.

MMDiT Editor

Multimodal diffusion transformer initialized from Stable Audio Open with v-prediction. A frozen Qwen2-Audio encoder provides conditioning; the source latent (64-d @ 21.5 Hz) is channel-concatenated at every denoising step for faithful non-target preservation. Inference uses 20 steps with CFG scale 5.

Progressive GRPO

Two-stage reinforcement learning (G = 4): Stage 1 optimizes the CoT planner with multi-rule rewards (λs=0.4, λv=0.3, λc=0.2, λe=0.1); Stage 2 optimizes the MMDiT editor with CLAP semantic and VGGish fidelity rewards, progressively shifting weight from semantics (0.7→0.3) to fidelity (0.3→0.7).

Figure 3. Overview of the quality-driven data curation pipeline and supported editing operations. GPT-4o converts AudioCaps/WavCaps captions into structured (input caption, edit instruction, output caption) triplets; Prompt-to-Prompt generation is optimized via Bayesian parameter search with a composite CLAP + mel-spectrogram objective (α = 0.5, 50 trials per pair).

Figure 4. Progressive GRPO post-training pipeline. Stage 1 optimizes the CoT planner with multi-rule rewards; Stage 2 optimizes the MMDiT editor with semantic and fidelity rewards.

Inference Efficiency Analysis

The full pipeline requires 9.85 s per 10 s sample on a single A100: ALM planning ~2.1 s (21.3%), RAG retrieval 0.3 s (3.0%), MMDiT generation (20 NFE) 7.1 s (72.1%), and encoding/post-processing 0.35 s (3.6%). Compared with AudioEditor (42.51 s) and MMEdit (19.28 s), the single-pass design achieves 4.3× and 2.0× speedups; consistency distillation can further reduce NFE to 4–6 steps. This latency suits offline creative workflows such as podcast post-processing.

RAG Retrieval Robustness

The planner incorporates graceful degradation: when maximum cosine similarity falls below τ = 0.6, the system reverts to zero-shot planning. Without RAG the model achieves FAD 3.59 (9.8% relative increase over 3.27). The 550K+ index spans six atomic operation types, providing sufficient coverage for common patterns; for rare long-tail compositions, performance degrades gracefully since the ALM retains decomposition capability independent of retrieval quality.

Directions for Future Improvement

The failure modes above motivate the following research directions:

R1 · Planner Robustness

Augment training with automatic instruction paraphrases (covering idiomatic, negated, and hedged phrasings) and add a paraphrase-invariance reward in Stage-1 GRPO to encourage semantically equivalent instructions to yield identical atomic plans.

R2 · Long-Tail Coverage

Expand the RAG index beyond the current 550K entries with rare-event corpora (FSD50K, Clotho-Detail). Replace the dense-only retriever with a hybrid dense + BM25 scheme so that uncommon sound-event terms are not dominated by embedding co-occurrence.

R3 · Event-Level Control

Introduce event-level mask supervision from a frozen sound-event detector during MMDiT training, plus an attention regularizer that penalizes non-target events being altered, to address dense-scene confusion.

R4 · Natural Blending

Add an acoustic-scene prior (room impulse response + SNR distribution learned from AudioSet) and a loudness-aware reward to Stage-2 GRPO, so that inserted events match the reverberation and level profile of the source scene.

On reporting failures. Consistent with recent community practice, transparent disclosure of failure modes is essential for reproducible research. All failure audios are produced by the released QuarkAudio-Edit checkpoint with default hyperparameters (20 denoising steps, CFG scale 5) to reflect real-world deployment behavior.
